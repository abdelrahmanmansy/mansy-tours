The Grand Egyptian Museum: a first-timer's guide

Tutankhamun's complete collection, a 4,600-year-old boat and 5,000 years of history under one roof. Here is how to see it without the overwhelm.

Ali Mansy12 May 20266 min read

The Grand Egyptian Museum is the largest archaeological museum on earth, and the first thing most visitors notice is not the gold — it is the scale. More than 100,000 artefacts, a grand staircase of royal statues and a view straight out to the pyramids. Trying to see it all in one go is the fastest way to remember none of it.

Start with Tutankhamun, then slow down

The boy king's galleries are the emotional heart of the museum. Go early, head straight there, and give yourself a full hour. The death mask alone is worth the trip, but it is the everyday objects — sandals, game boards, a lock of hair — that make him feel human. After that, take a break. The museum is a marathon, not a sprint.

Do not skip the Khufu boat

The 4,600-year-old solar boat of Khufu has its own building and almost no queue. It is 43 metres of cedar, reassembled from 1,200 pieces, and it was buried beside the Great Pyramid to carry the pharaoh into the afterlife. Most visitors walk right past it. Do not be most visitors.

The practical bits

Book a timed ticket online, arrive at opening and allow at least four hours. The museum is about 20 minutes from central Cairo and 10 minutes from the pyramids, so pairing it with Giza in one day is possible but ambitious. A private guide pays for itself here — not for the facts, but for the route.

“The best trips in Egypt are not the busiest ones. They are the ones with time to sit still.”
